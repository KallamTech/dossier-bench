## 2. Key issues identified

2.1 Validity of termination. Clause 12.1 requires written notice specifying the breach and a 21-day cure period before termination for a remediable breach. No such notice was served; the 2 May letter terminates with immediate effect. Late completion is plainly capable of remedy. SA's preliminary view: termination was wrongful and amounts to a repudiatory breach which Tidewell can accept, or Tidewell can treat the letter as a termination for convenience under clause 12.3 (30 days' notice, payment for work done plus 10% demobilisation). Need to elect carefully; advise within two weeks.

2.2 Extension of time. Both notices appear to be within the 14-day period in clause 9.2. Floor slab is Brisamar's responsibility under clause 6.2; power supply under clause 6.4. Need a delay expert to quantify the critical delay and separate out Tidewell's own delays (DM accepted delivery was six days late and that Velden's software had defects in April).

2.3 Liquidated damages. Brisamar claims €45,080 (seven weeks from 21 March). LDs under clause 10.1 run from the practical completion date, not mechanical completion, so the calculation is wrong on its face. With an extension of time the exposure should be small.

2.4 Withholding of invoice 0417. Clause 3.5 requires notice five business days before the due date (23 April). The withholding notice came on 2 May, after the due date. Interest at 4% over base runs from 23 April.

2.5 Brisamar's survey. Brisamar has asserted its own survey shows the slab within tolerance but has never produced it. Request it in pre-action correspondence.
